02 // why this matters

Engineers Aren't Short on Tools. They're Short on Understanding.

Teams run old and new pipelines side by side for months or years. Someone has to understand both. We teach the middle. We don't teach tools; we build mental models, so engineers can see the system behind every ticket.

04 // how we teach

We Don't Teach Software. We Teach Engineers.

The goal isn't memorizing commands. It's understanding that transfers across tools, teams, and career stages.

◎

Mental Models First

Every module opens with a model of how the system behaves, then proves it in a lab. Tools change every few years. The model transfers.

▤

Modular by Design

Two-hour modules, each with a hands-on lab. Combine them into full-day or half-day sessions that fit your team's calendar.

⟳

Labs That Build on Each Other

In multi-day courses, labs build toward one capstone, with checkpoint repos so anyone who falls behind can rejoin without starting over.

◇

Built for the Messy Middle

Legacy CI/CD, GitOps, and the migrations in between, taught inside real enterprise constraints: compliance, change management, and systems you can't turn off.

08 // about

Where Engineers Rehearse Before Production

In software, staging is where you rehearse before production. Staging Ground is that place for engineers: hands-on labs where teams practice on real-shaped systems before the stakes are real.

Staging Ground was founded by Dwayne Pugh, a systems engineer and technical educator with more than a decade in production across government, automotive technology, critical infrastructure, and enterprise platforms.

He started on the help desk, closing tickets. The jump to Tier 2 production support taught him the lesson this company is built on: a ticket is a symptom of a system. The strongest engineers don't know more tools. They ask one more question: what else touches this, and when?
